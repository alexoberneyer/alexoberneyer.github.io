02 / Start with less

Why is it so difficult to let things go?

The obstacles are often psychological rather than practical. Naming them makes decisions easier without requiring anyone to be ruthless.

01

Loss aversion

Giving something away feels like a loss even when keeping it brings no real benefit. Ask: “What do I lose by keeping it?”

02

The endowment effect

We value objects more simply because we own them. Ask: “Would I buy this again today?”

03

Sunk costs

A high past purchase price can keep an unused item on the shelf. That money is already spent; the decision now is about space and usefulness.

04

Aspirational identity

We keep equipment for the person we hope to become. Keep a realistic, bounded amount for active goals rather than every possible future self.

05

Sentimental attachment

A memory matters even when the object is bulky. Consider a photo or a small memory box, but keep irreplaceable treasures intentionally.

06

Decision fatigue

Too many choices drain energy. Begin with easy categories, work in short sessions, and defer genuinely difficult items to a dated uncertainty box.

Three rules for deciding together

Own your own decisions

Never discard another adult's belongings without their agreement.

Two yeses for shared items

Discuss shared possessions without pressure. If either person is unsure, set a review date.

Make leaving easy

Set up donation bags, pick a drop-off location, and schedule the trip before sorting.

A 90-day uncertainty box helps with reversible decisions: date the box, keep it out of sight, then deliberately review its contents. Do not use this rule for important documents or irreplaceable objects.

04 / Put it into practice

A realistic two-week start.

Don't reorganize your whole home in a weekend. Begin with two 45–60-minute decluttering sessions, then establish small, repeatable routines.
